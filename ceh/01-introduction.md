# Module 01 - Introduction to Ethical Hacking

## Understanding Different Types of Attacks (Simple Terms)

Cyber attacks are methods used to steal data, interrupt services, or gain unauthorized access to systems. These attacks can happen in different ways.

---

## 1. Active Attacks

Active attacks happen when an attacker directly interacts with the target system or network.  
They send traffic, modify data, or disrupt services. These attacks are usually detectable.

### Examples:
- Denial of Service (DoS) – making a website or server unavailable
- Malware – viruses, worms, ransomware
- Password attacks – guessing or stealing passwords
- Session hijacking – taking over an active login session
- Man-in-the-Middle – intercepting communication between two users
- DNS / ARP poisoning – redirecting traffic to fake systems
- SQL Injection – attacking databases through web forms
- Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) – injecting malicious scripts into websites
- Privilege escalation – gaining admin access
- Backdoor access – hidden unauthorized access

### Key Point:
These attacks try to damage, control, or exploit systems actively.

---

## 2. Close-in Attacks

Close-in attacks happen when the attacker is physically near the victim or target system.

### Examples:
- Shoulder surfing – watching someone type passwords
- Eavesdropping – listening to confidential conversations
- Dumpster diving – collecting sensitive information from trash
- Social engineering – tricking users into sharing information

### Key Point:
These attacks rely on physical presence and human weakness.

---

## 3. Insider Attacks

Insider attacks are performed by trusted people inside the organization such as employees, contractors, or vendors.

Because insiders already have access, these attacks are dangerous and difficult to detect.

### Examples:
- Stealing company data
- Misusing admin privileges
- Copying confidential files
- Damaging systems intentionally
- Wiretapping internal communication

### Key Point:
Internal threats can affect confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data.

---

## 4. Distribution Attacks

Distribution attacks happen before hardware or software reaches the customer.

Attackers modify products during manufacturing or shipping.

### Examples:
- Installing backdoors in devices
- Modifying software before delivery
- Compromised supply chain products

### Key Point:
Even trusted hardware/software can be dangerous if tampered with earlier.

---

# Information Warfare (InfoWar)

Information Warfare means using technology and information systems to gain advantage over an opponent.

Instead of physical weapons, attackers use digital tools.

### Common Weapons:
- Viruses
- Worms
- Trojan horses
- Logic bombs
- Penetration tools
- Electronic jamming

---

## Types of Information Warfare

### 1. Command and Control Warfare
Taking control of enemy systems or networks.

### 2. Intelligence-Based Warfare
Collecting information using sensors or surveillance systems.

### 3. Electronic Warfare
Disrupting communication systems using radio or electronic signals.

### 4. Psychological Warfare
Using fear, propaganda, or misinformation.

### 5. Hacker Warfare
Using hacking tools to steal data, damage systems, or monitor activity.

### 6. Economic Warfare
Disrupting business or national economy through cyber means.

### 7. Cyber Warfare
Large-scale attacks using information systems against individuals, organizations, or nations.

---

# Defensive vs Offensive Information Warfare

### Defensive
Protecting systems, networks, and information from attacks.

### Offensive
Launching attacks against the opponent’s systems.

---

# What is Hacking?

Hacking means finding weaknesses in systems or applications and using them to gain access or control.

It may be done for:

- Learning and testing
- Security improvement
- Stealing data
- Financial fraud
- Causing disruption

---

# Key Takeaway

Cybersecurity professionals learn hacking methods to understand risks and secure systems before attackers exploit them.
